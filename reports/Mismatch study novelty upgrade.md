# Predict the transition, then act on it

The study's novelty gap is not that the pendulum is a toy. It is that H1, and much of H2, can be computed from the design without running the grid: the nominal ensemble σ, MC dropout and the nominal-ODE residual never see true-system data, so the RQ1 "dose-response in r" is a mixing curve between how well σ tracks the approximation error a and how well it tracks the discrepancy d. Meanwhile 2025-2026 position papers already state the bare claim that epistemic estimators miss bias ([2505.23506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23506); [2608.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13633)). The highest-value upgrade turns that weakness into the paper's main claim. **Predict r* in advance from a handful of quantities measured at ε = 0 on pilot seed 0, freeze the prediction, and test it on held-out seeds, n_nom levels and systems.** Then turn the measured transition into a method: a gate that switches acquisition rule once an observable estimate r̂ crosses it, plus synthetic-powered conformal intervals that stay valid with 8-16 true points. These three upgrades (copula theory for RQ1, discrepancy-augmented SPI, r-gated arm 8) cost about a week each and almost no extra fits, so they fit the late-January workshop draft. Four more belong in the March paper: an exact linear-smoother theory for RQ2 that predicts the sign of the S2 mismatch in H4, a Goodhart-style acquisition-concentration law, the exact misspecification/(de-)amplification decomposition, and one PDE system on Exponax. Everything new has to go into a single `prereg-v1.1` addendum, with its own Holm family, committed before the pilot is analysed (planned for Nov 25-Dec 1). My judgement, not a measurement: the v3 scope plausibly moves novelty from about 50 to about 60-65 and significance from about 45 to about 52-57, with rigor held near 80, provided the predictions are frozen before data and Family C stays small.

## H1 is computable from the design, and reviewers will say so first

The reviewer objections, ranked by how much they hurt, are as follows. The worst is that **H1 is true by construction**: nominal σ is the same function of θ in every ε cell, so AUROC(σ, ‖e‖) slides from its ε = 0 value toward AUROC(σ, ‖d‖) as r grows, and the endpoint is fixed by where each missing term lives (high velocity for S1's drag, low amplitude for S2's friction). The second is that the central claim is published in general form. Jiménez, Jürgens and Waegeman argue that approximation error and estimation bias are absent from existing epistemic estimators, and report bias "frequently larger than –and largely uncorrelated with–" the epistemic estimates across 8 UQ methods ([2505.23506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23506)). The PHYSTAT community report lists structural misspecification (missing terms) as its own category and says simulation-trained models "inherit their misspecifications" ([2608.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13633)). Third, active learning under misspecification already has theory. Tang, Sloman and Kaski split test error into misspecification bias, estimation error and an error-(de-)amplification cross term, and show expected-information-gain design doing worse than random under misspecification ([2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)). Fourth, prior evidence says **H2 will be null**. Koermer et al. varied bias at three levels and found the "pecking order" of design methods unchanged ([2301.10228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10228)), and Tang et al. add a second three-level sweep ([2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)).

The rest hurt less but will come up. Sim-to-real RL already spends real samples only inside an estimated mismatch region, with guarantees under a separation assumption ([2609.01418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01418)). r is not observable before experiments are chosen, so a practitioner cannot act on it. A frictionless pendulum against a "real" pendulum with friction is a standard toy ([2405.08719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08719)), and a 2026 sim-to-sim pendulum budget paper uses the same 10-seed, paired-bootstrap style ([2606.22062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22062)). Reviewers will also ask for baselines: R-IDeA and a "system-identify first" arm. Continuously widening simulation gaps are already studied in SBI ([2406.03154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03154)).

Three claims survive all of this. **No paper found derives the bias-to-approximation ratio at which a variance-type signal loses its ranking of true error, or the bias level at which variance-driven acquisition falls behind space-filling design, or tests whether the two coincide.** The closest results are worst-case size bounds: confidence-width inflation and Ω(εT) regret in misspecified GP bandits ([2111.05008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05008)), the RKHS-distance term in misspecified kriging ([2312.09225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09225)), an average-size phase transition between posterior variance and risk ([2306.03783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03783)), and conformal coverage-gap bounds ([2202.13415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415)). None of them concerns per-input ranking. The second surviving claim is the distinction a reviewer may miss and the paper must argue. In Tang et al. the learner is misspecified; here the correction learner is flexible, and only the acquisition score comes from the misspecified nominal model ([2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)). The third is that arm 7, the nominal-ODE residual of observed true trajectories regressed on θ as an acquisition score for a surrogate's correction model, does not appear in the literature found. Its neighbours each own one piece of it: UDE plus symbolic-regression model discrimination ([2604.01231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01231)), E-SINDy residual-times-disagreement scores ([2606.12182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12182)), residual diagnosis without acquisition ([2606.23215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23215)) and proxy-corrected acquisition for failure search ([2608.13719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719)).

## Three January upgrades: predict r½, cover the true system, gate the rule

All three can be built in weeks 2-3 (Nov 11-24) alongside the planned code, specified in the addendum before the pilot, and run on the S1 grid in December.

### Upgrade 1: Model A predicts RQ1's transition from five pilot numbers

**What matters.** Treat (log‖a‖², log‖g‖², log s) as trivariate Gaussian, where s is the signal and d = εg. Linearise log‖e‖² at the medians with weight w = 1/(1 + r²). This gives the ranking correlation ρ(r) as a closed-form function of five quantities measured at ε = 0: the two log-spreads, the baseline skill corr(s, a), the alignment corr(s, g) and corr(a, g). In the simplest case (no alignment, independent errors) the midpoint is **r½ = 3^{1/4}·(s_A/s_D)^{1/2} ≈ 1.32·(s_A/s_D)^{1/2}**. The model also predicts a Hill slope near 2 in log r. Both results are the research notes' own derivations, not published, and the algebra needs checking. Inputs come from a decomposition consistent with Tang et al.'s Proposition 1 ([2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)), and kriging posterior variance does not contain the misspecification term ([2312.09225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09225)). The model gives three falsifiable predictions: r½ per system, a fitted 4PL slope near 2 per natural-log unit, and r-collapse across n_nom **if and only if** the spreads, baseline skill and error correlation do not change with n_nom. The last can be checked at ε = 0 before any ε > 0 cell is examined. The model also gives an exact coverage formula for σ-scaled intervals that uses only ε = 0 quantities plus d.

**Can the budget detect it?** The criterion "predicted r½ inside the empirical 90% bootstrap CI and within a factor of 1.5" reuses the pre-registered ±log 1.5 margin and the 10 seeds already planned, so it adds no fits. Most likely outcome (a guess): the shape and slope hold roughly on S1, and the midpoint is off by more than the margin wherever error spreads are heavy-tailed. A fully empirical Monte-Carlo plug-in, which recombines the saved σ_i and d_i, separates copula error from linearisation error, so a miss is still informative.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1. Closed form plus Monte-Carlo plug-in, inputs from pilot seed 0 at n_nom = 512 | Real out-of-sample prediction; cheap | Assumptions may fail | ~1 week, CPU only |
| A2. Monte-Carlo plug-in only | Fewer assumptions | Weaker as theory ("you just computed it") | ~3 days |
| A3. Skip theory; report curves | Zero cost | Leaves objection O2 standing | 0 |

**Recommendation:** A1. Switch to A2 if pilot seed 0 shows log-spreads well above 1 or a large cosine between a and g, since the linearisation then cannot be trusted. **Unknown until the pilot:** whether the marginals are near-lognormal, the size of the a·g cosine, and whether baseline skill drifts with n_nom. All are measurable on pilot seed 0 in under an hour. **Pre-registration:** add it to `prereg-v1.1` as prediction P-A. Name the pilot-seed-0 inputs, the predicted quantities, the success criterion, and a clarification of "loses half its ranking ability" as the 4PL midpoint between plateaus, consistent with B2. Compute the predicted numbers from pilot seed 0 and commit them (for example as tag `pred-v1`) before any seed 1-10 result is opened. Seed 0 is used only as input, so its exclusion from analysis stands.

### Upgrade 2: Discrepancy-augmented SPI gives valid coverage with 8 true points

**What matters.** The pre-registered true-only conformal signal (signal 5) breaks at the small end of its own range. At 90% coverage, split conformal needs n ≥ 9, so at n_cal = 8 the interval is trivially infinite. SPI notes this regime explicitly: standard split conformal at m + 1 < 1/α must output trivial sets ([2505.13432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13432)). SPI calibrates on abundant synthetic scores through a quantile-mapping transporter. It keeps a worst-case guardrail that holds even when the synthetic data depend on the real calibration points (Theorem 3.5), and its slack is a total-variation gap between real and synthetic score distributions (Theorem 3.3), with experiments at m = 15 ([2505.13432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13432)). The upgrade uses nominal simulations as the synthetic source and augments them with discrepancies sampled from the discrepancy ensemble fitted on the same n true points. This is the learned analogue of Byun et al.'s assumed-GP augmentation ([2610.31163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31163)). The r dial then tests Theorem 3.3 directly: the TV gap should widen with r for plain SPI and stay flatter for augmented SPI. TCC's impossibility theorem, that label-free transfer must be near-vacuous in the worst case, gives the citable reason true-system points are needed at all ([2609.10737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10737)). No application of SPI to a physics surrogate was found.

**Can the budget detect it?** The comparison with true-only conformal at n_cal = 8 is guaranteed to be detectable, because one side is infinite. That makes it a correct but weak result, so do not lead with it. The test that carries information is C3: the slope of interval width against log r for augmented versus plain SPI. That slope comparison has unknown power. Most likely outcome: coverage holds near 0.9 on all cells, and the plain-versus-augmented slope difference is modest.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1. Plain + augmented SPI at n_cal ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64}, post hoc on RQ1 models | Validity independent of H2; public code (github.com/Meshiba/spi) | Trajectory-norm scores may widen transporter windows | ~1 week, ~0 fits |
| S2. Plain SPI only | Simplest | No dose-response test; less novel | ~3 days |
| S3. Add Donati-style directional score ([2607.29344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29344)) | Asymmetric bands | Needs a split of scarce true points; likely no gain at n = 8 | +3 days |

**Recommendation:** S1. Drop to S2 if the pilot shows transporter windows covering most of the score range at m = 8. **Unknown until the pilot:** window width at m = 8 with trajectory-valued scores; measure it post hoc on the 3 pilot seeds. **Pre-registration:** Family C tests C2 (coverage ≥ 0.9 minus a stated tolerance, with width below true-only conformal at n_cal ∈ {8, 16}) and C3 (the slope contrast), with β fixed in the addendum (SPI used β = 0.4).

### Upgrade 3: Arm 8 makes r observable and turns the transition into a decision rule

**What matters.** r needs f_true on a test set, so a practitioner cannot compute it. Here, though, the nominal simulator can be run at every queried θ. The numerator of r̂ comes from the n observed true points, debiased by the known σ_obs. The denominator, the median over the pool of ‖f̂ − f_nom‖, costs about 4,096 nominal solves (roughly 90 s per round at 0.0215 s per solve). Arm 8 uses nominal σ (arm 3) while the bootstrap CI's upper end is below r_switch, and switches once the lower end exceeds it. Published analogues gate on a measured statistic but never switch an acquisition rule on a ratio of structural to approximation error. TCC-KS converts a mismatch certificate into a tighter α ([2609.10737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10737)). Parashar et al.'s control-variate weight β collapses to about 0 at proxy-target correlation ρ ≈ 0.15 on nuPlan ([2608.13719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719)). R-IDeA's authors speculate that their hyperparameter should adapt to the degree of misspecification ([2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)). Schmitt et al. show that an MMD score with N = 5 real series already detects misspecification ([2406.03154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03154)). A gate on r̂ is therefore likely to be detectable at n = 8, but this is not yet measured here.

**Can the budget detect it?** The success metric is per-seed budget-AUC regret against the per-r best fixed arm, with arm 8 below every fixed arm's regret. Its power depends on the between-seed SD of the regret contrast, which is unknown. **Most likely outcome: "ties the best arm".** Koermer's null ([2301.10228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10228)) and the finding that residual and worst-error rules can hurt with no mismatch at all ([2610.27130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27130)) both suggest the fixed arms may not diverge enough for a gate to pay off. "Never loses badly anywhere" is still a reportable, honest result.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1. Two-way gate (arm 3 → arm 7), S1 in January, S2 in March | Cheapest; directly uses H4's r½ | Fails if arm 7 loses to Sobol | +1,500 fits S1 (4-25 CPU-h), ~1 week |
| G2. Three-way gate (arm 3 → Sobol → arm 7) | Safe against arm 7's outlier oversampling | Two thresholds to freeze; more tuning surface | Same fits, +2 days |
| G3. Continuous trust weight (Parashar-style β) | No hard threshold | Less tied to the measured transition | Same fits, +3 days |

**Recommendation:** G2. It costs the same as G1 and protects against arm 7 losing to Sobol. Freeze r_switch at r = 1 rather than at a pilot-estimated r½, so that the pilot does not tune the method on its own target. Switch to G1 if the pilot shows arm 7 beating Sobol at every ε > 0. **Unknown until the pilot:** the sampling spread of r̂ at n = 8 under 1% and 5% noise, and the regret SD; both can be computed post hoc on the pilot seeds at no extra fit cost. **Pre-registration:** Family C test C1 (arm 8 regret < minimum fixed-arm regret, per system, paired t across seeds), with thresholds, CI method and switch logic frozen in the addendum.

## March upgrades: exact RQ2 theory, an acquisition law, one PDE

### Upgrade 4: Model B predicts the sign of the H4 failure on S2

**What matters.** Replace the discrepancy ensemble with the arm-6 KRR/GP, with hyperparameters frozen at ε_ref. For a static design, the expected IMSE is then exactly Q_aa + 2εQ_ag + ε²Q_gg + N: quadratic forms in the saved arrays, each a 56×56 solve (milliseconds on CPU). Arms 3 and 4 never see true data, and GP posterior variance does not depend on y ([2312.09225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09225)), so these arms are fixed designs. Their gap to Sobol is therefore a ratio of quadratics in ε. It is logistic-like in log ε with slope 2, its midpoint is at an effective ratio r_eff ≈ 1, and the crossover is the positive root of a quadratic.

Model B makes two predictions that are not obvious. First, H2 is predicted null whenever the ratio of an arm's Q_gg to Sobol's matches its ratio of approximation-error terms. That is a candidate explanation of Koermer's null with a single smooth sinusoidal bias ([2301.10228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10228)); it has not been checked against their numbers. Second, **H4 should hold on S1 and fail on S2 in a predicted direction.** S2's near-nonsmooth friction leaves more unexplained per unit norm, so RQ2's transition should come at a smaller raw r than RQ1's. Offset-transfer theory supports the mechanism: target-sample cost depends on the offset's Sobolev smoothness and norm, not on its L2 size ([2402.14966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14966)). This is also why the pre-registered H¹/L2 smoothness proxy is the right covariate.

**Can the budget detect it?** The S1 check against the MLP correction can run on December data. The S2 sign prediction needs the S2 grid, which is scheduled for January in the background, so the S2 test lands in March. The sign is a binary prediction with 10 seeds of bootstrap CI on the midpoint difference, which is easier to detect than equivalence. Most likely outcome: the KRR curve tracks the MLP qualitatively, with offsets. Adaptive arms (5, 7, refitted 6) fall outside the formula and are reported as deviations from a reference curve.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1. Freeze Model B predictions in v1.1, implement in December, S1 check in workshop appendix if ready, S2 test in March | Prediction is genuinely prior; workshop not blocked | Two weeks of December work during the S1 grid | 2-3 weeks, CPU minutes |
| B2. Model B only in March, frozen in a v1.2 addendum before S2 is analysed | Lighter December | S1 data seen first, so the S1 part becomes postdiction | Same |
| B3. B1 plus an empirical-NTK kernel swap | Closer to the MLP | +1-2 days, extra knob | +2 days |

**Recommendation:** B1. The H4 sign prediction is the strongest single result available in the notes, and it is only worth anything if it is frozen before S2 data exist. Drop to B2 if the S1 grid slips past Dec 29. **Unknown until the pilot:** how well a frozen-hyperparameter KRR predicts an MLP ensemble's corrected error at n ≤ 56. **Pre-registration:** prediction P-B in v1.1 states the plateau signs for arms 3 and 4, the crossover root, and the direction of the S2 mismatch.

### Upgrade 5: A Goodhart law for acquisition concentration

**What matters.** The batch exponent k in p ∝ score^k is an optimisation-pressure knob. Map the nominal score to a proxy reward and the true-error reduction to a gold reward, and the setting matches reward-model overoptimisation. Gao, Schulman and Hilton fit gold reward as d(α − βd) against d = √KL ([2210.10760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)), and Aouad et al. find gain Θ(√min{log M, K}) with R² of 0.95-0.99 and name adaptive collection as future work ([2609.38526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38526)). The prediction is an inverted U in gain against √KL(q_k‖uniform pool), with the peak falling as r rises and the curve becoming monotone decreasing at large r. That gives a positive, structured result even if H2 is null, and the claim generalises to any field with a simulator. No application of Goodhart scaling to experimental design was found after one search, which is an absence of evidence rather than proof.

**Can the budget detect it?** The full sweep is 7 k values × 5 ε × 10 seeds × 6 rounds × 5 members = 10,500 fits, about +40%. With the other additions, the full sweep would push total compute past the pre-registered 425 CPU-h ceiling at the upper fit-time estimate. Greedy batches add between-seed noise. Most likely outcome: unknown. The functional forms come from LLM settings, and the curve may simply be flat.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1. Pilot k ∈ {0, 1, 4, greedy}; if non-monotone at any ε, run a reduced main sweep (4 k × 3 ε × 10 seeds, arm 3, S1) | Pilot-gated; ~3,600 fits | Coarse KL grid | 10-60 CPU-h, 1-2 weeks |
| K2. Full 7-k sweep | Best curve fits | +10,500 fits; breaks the compute ceiling at upper estimates | 29-175 CPU-h |
| K3. Skip | No cost | Loses the most general framing | 0 |

**Recommendation:** K1, run in January-February for March. Switch to K3 if the pilot curve is flat in k at every ε. Note that the pilot also sets the frozen k for arms 3-7 from {1, 2, 4}. The sweep must leave that choice untouched and be labelled exploratory. **Unknown until the pilot:** the gain variance at high k, and whether KL over a 4,096-candidate pool is a stable pressure measure. **Pre-registration:** add it to v1.1 as an exploratory block with the gate condition written down. It has no confirmatory test.

### Upgrade 6: Compute the (de-)amplification term exactly

Because f_true is known, the corrected error can be split exactly into misspecification bias, estimation error and Tang et al.'s cross term A ([2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)) for every arm and r. The only addition is one large-n "best-in-class" δ fit per cell, about 100-300 fits. This answers objection O4 with data: it shows whether the S1-versus-S2 difference is a de-amplification effect. There is little risk and no detection problem, since it is descriptive. Record it as exploratory in v1.1. It can go in the workshop appendix if time allows, and belongs in the March paper.

### Upgrade 7: One PDE system answers "toy ODE"

**What matters.** Recent misspecification and multi-fidelity papers use canonical PDEs: diffusion-reaction, Burgers and cavity flow ([2606.03469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03469)), and modified Darcy and smoke inflow ([2608.04708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04708)). ML4PS 2025 accepted several closure and surrogate-UQ papers ([ML4PS 2025](https://ml4physicalsciences.github.io/2025)). Exponax (MIT licence) covers Burgers, KdV, KS and reaction-diffusion with physical-coefficient steppers ([Exponax](https://github.com/Ceyron/exponax)). That makes S3 = 1D Burgers → KdV-Burgers (+ε·u_xxx dispersion) a cheap PDE analogue. Dispersion puts the discrepancy in high wavenumbers, a different spectral signature from the approximation error. A combined stepper under that name is not confirmed in the Exponax API.

**Can the budget detect it?** On a reduced grid (1 system × 3 ε × 4 arms × 10 seeds), S3 is about 3,600 fits, comparable to the pendulum per fit only if outputs are reduced to about 32-64 PCA modes. A raw-field MLP could exceed the 60 s fit ceiling. Every runtime here is an unverified estimate; Exponax states only a GPU figure ([Exponax](https://github.com/Ceyron/exponax)). Most likely outcome: S3 reproduces the qualitative RQ1 transition. RQ2 on S3 is as uncertain as on S1. Arm 7 becomes harder, because it needs spatial derivatives of noisy snapshots.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1. Burgers → KdV-Burgers on Exponax, reduced grid, March | Familiar PDE; ε dial kept | New stepper and PCA pipeline | ~1 day pilot + 2-3 weeks; 10-60 CPU-h |
| T2. Fisher-KPP with a missing reaction term | Matches 2606.03469's testbeds | Discrepancy closer to an additive field | Same |
| T3. Battery SPM → SPMe/DFN in PyBaMM ([Ecker notebook](https://docs.pybamm.org/en/stable/source/examples/notebooks/models/compare-ecker-data.html)) | Physically meaningful, natural r from C-rate | New domain; solver quirks; licence and timings unverified | 3-4 weeks |

**Recommendation:** T1 for March. **Do not add it before the S1 workshop cut**: the plan already moves S2 into the background, and a third system before the S1 pilot passes puts the January date at risk. Switch to T2 if the KdV-Burgers stepper cannot be built with the generic API in a day. NeuroBEM real quadrotor residuals ([readme](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/neuro_bem/Readme.html)) are a single-r, RQ1-only external-validity case. They come with model-predicted and residual force columns, but no licence is stated, so email the authors first. **Unknown until the pilot:** CPU solve time, fit time with PCA outputs, and where r = 1 falls in ε; a one-day Exponax pilot measures all three. **Pre-registration:** S3 is not in section 3. Pre-register it as a separate `prereg-v1.2` addendum (system, ε grid, arms, exploratory or secondary status) before its pilot is analysed, and log it in section 11.

## One addendum, committed before the pilot is analysed

The pilot has not run, so an amendment is still clean, but only once. Write a single `prereg-v1.1` addendum and commit it by **Nov 24**, before the Nov 25 pilot. Log it in section 11 with the date, what changed and why. The addendum has five parts. (1) The predictions: P-A (Model A: inputs, predicted r½ and slope per system, collapse condition, success criterion) and P-B (Model B: plateau signs, crossover, S2 mismatch direction). (2) A new **Family C with its own Holm correction**, made up of C1 (gate regret), C2 (SPI coverage and width at n_cal ∈ {8, 16}) and C3 (SPI width-slope contrast). Families A and B stay untouched, so the original confirmatory claims keep their power. (3) The frozen gate logic, with r_switch = 1, and SPI's β. ## Conclusion

The project's most valuable asset is something the field usually lacks: an exact split of every error into its approximation and structural parts, with the simulator available at every point. That turns "blindness exists" (already published) into "blindness is predictable from five numbers at ε = 0" (not found anywhere), and turns an unobservable dial into an observable switch. The likely null on H2 changes from a threat into a test of Model B, which says when acquisition gaps should not diverge, and why.

The deadline decision is simple. Everything that needs only saved arrays, post-hoc calibration or one extra arm on S1 goes in January. Everything that needs S2 data, new fits at scale or a new solver goes in March. None of it is credible unless `prereg-v1.1` is committed before Nov 25. Record this decision and its reason in the `project_goal_v2.md` changelog.
