## Q1. Mismatch-aware acquisition: has anyone used the residual of a known-but-incomplete ODE, evaluated on measured data, as an acquisition score for a surrogate?

### Takeaway
I found no paper that does exactly what arm 7 does: compute the nominal-ODE residual on observed true trajectories, regress it on the parameters θ, and use the prediction to choose experiments for a neural surrogate's correction model. The nearest work splits into two groups. One uses the nominal-model residual on data only to diagnose where the model is wrong (LISDD). The other designs experiments to discover missing physics, but inside the system-identification setting rather than a surrogate over θ: UDE plus symbolic-regression model discrimination (Strouwen), and E-SINDy residual-times-disagreement scores (Larrañaga, Fasel, Brunton). A proxy-corrected acquisition that adds a learned sim-to-real residual to the proxy signal also exists, but for failure discovery (Parashar et al.). So arm 7 is new as an acquisition rule for surrogates. The delta over the closest work is narrow, and that delta is the paper's claim.

### Cited Findings
- **Strouwen & Miclută-Câmpeanu, "Experimental Design for Missing Physics" (arXiv:2604.01231, IFAC/DYCOPS 2025, read in full).** The missing physics μ(C_s) of a fed-batch bioreactor is represented by a neural network inside a universal differential equation, then post-processed by symbolic regression into the top M = 10 candidate expressions. The next experiment (a piecewise-constant feed-rate control) maximises the average pairwise max-squared difference between the candidates' predicted outputs, a variant of T-optimal model discrimination. After 3 designed experiments Monod kinetics topped the hall of fame. Across 5 random-control replicates of 3 experiments each, Monod was never recovered. The only baseline is random. There is no surrogate over parameters, no sweep of missing-term strength and no space-filling comparator, and the criterion uses model predictions, not residuals on measured data. — [arXiv:2604.01231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01231)
- **Larrañaga, Fasel, Brunton, "How Low Can You Go? Active Learning for Sparse Model Discovery in the Ultra-Low-Data Limit" (arXiv:2606.12182, read).** E-SINDy bootstrap ensemble. For ODEs, query-by-committee selects initial conditions with maximum ensemble variance of predicted dynamics, with a distance filter for diversity. For PDEs, an "ensemble score" s_i = μ̃_i·ρ̃_i, where μ_i is the mean absolute residual |∂_t u_i − θ̃_iᵀξ^(k)| of the current SINDy models on the data and ρ_i is its coefficient of variation; this "combines model error and ensemble disagreement". Baselines are random and D-optimal. Reported: Lorenz identified with fewer than 50 points, Burgers with fewer than 24, KS with fewer than 100. Stated limitation: active learning helps most when the domain mixes informative and uninformative regions. Delta from arm 7: the residual comes from the learned library model, not a fixed nominal model with a missing term; the goal is equation discovery, not surrogate accuracy over θ; there is no mismatch dial. — [arXiv:2606.12182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12182)
- **Parashar et al. (MIT/NVIDIA), "Coverage Aware Active Evaluation for Failure Discovery with Paired Systems" (arXiv:2608.13719, read).** The acquisition is α(x) = μ_CV(x) + support-aware mutual information. μ_CV is a local control-variate estimator that corrects proxy (simulator) failure signals with a surrogate q_θ trained on target-system evaluations. β = (k/(k+n))·Var(Y_s)⁻¹Cov(Y_r, Y_s) shrinks toward 0 when proxy-target correlation is weak: on nuPlan, average ρ ≈ 0.15 drove β → 0 with small gains, while on KITTI, ρ ≈ 0.5 gave clearer gains. Up to 2x more failures than random and active-learning baselines. The target budget is small (e.g. Quadruped: 10 initial + 20 acquired, 2 seeds; nuPlan: 20 + 150, 4 seeds). This is the closest "simulator signal + learned sim-to-real correction → acquisition" paper, but its goal is failure discovery (exploitation), not surrogate accuracy, and it has no mismatch-strength sweep. — [arXiv:2608.13719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719)
- **Tang, Sloman, Kaski, "Representative, Informative, and De-Amplifying" (arXiv:2506.07805, AISTATS 2026, read).** Decomposes generalisation error under misspecification into misspecification bias + estimation bias + an "error (de-)amplification" cross term 2E[(f̄ − f*)(f̂ − f̄)]. Proposes R-I (EIG × MMD-representativeness ratio) and R-IDeA (adds a sigmoid de-amplification term built from a learned adversarial proxy g). Under misspecification, EIG-only BAD can do worse than random. In the polynomial toy (Fig. 10), R-IDeA's error ratio to random stays below 1 as misspecification grows, but its advantage shrinks. Their misspecification is a wrong model class for Bayesian parameter inference, not a simulator missing a term with a learned additive correction. — [arXiv:2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)
- **Wang, LISDD (arXiv:2606.23215).** Already read by the user (week1 file): nominal-model residual on observed data, used to localise and test a sparse missing term with a sample-split F-test; diagnosis only, no acquisition. — [arXiv:2606.23215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23215)
- Search also returned, **[abstract/title only]**: "Data-Efficient Neural Operator Training via Physics-Based Active Learning" (arXiv:2605.21348, UCL/UKAEA); "Bayesian Optimization of Partially Known Systems using Hybrid Models" (arXiv:2603.11199); "Bayesian Inference for Missing Physics" (arXiv:2603.14918, likely the same Strouwen line); "Orthogonal Discrepancy Kernels for Learning with Partial Physics" (arXiv:2606.21199); "Physics-guided correction for operator learning under model misspecification" (arXiv:2606.03469). None was read; 2605.21348 is the one to check before submission, since "physics-based AL for neural operators" could use a PDE-residual score. — [arXiv:2605.21348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21348)

### Inferences
- The defensible novelty statement for arm 7: "the nominal-ODE residual of observed true trajectories, regressed on θ, as an acquisition score for a neural surrogate's discrepancy model, evaluated across a dense mismatch dial against Sobol". Strouwen (model discrimination via UDE+SR), Larrañaga (E-SINDy residual × disagreement), LISDD (residual for diagnosis) and Parashar (control-variate proxy correction for failure search) each own one piece. All four must be cited.
- A method contribution that goes beyond arm 7 should combine the two error sources rather than pick one. The project's own decomposition e = a + d maps onto Tang et al.'s decomposition, and onto the E-SINDy score's "model error × disagreement" product. One natural candidate is an expected-total-error-reduction score: (approximation variance from the nominal ensemble) + (predicted discrepancy magnitude and its variance from arm 7's GP). Caveat: Romor et al. (2610.27130, in the user's week-1 notes) found that error-seeking acquisition oversamples outliers at small budgets, so a pure "go where |d̂| is large" score may lose to Sobol even when it sees the missing term.
- Parashar's β collapsing to 0 under weak proxy-target correlation is a built-in gate. It is the same mechanism as the "switch rule" idea in Q3, which is evidence that an adaptive trust weight on simulator-only signals is a reasonable design.

### Gaps
- 2605.21348, 2603.11199, 2606.21199 and 2606.03469 were not read; any of them could contain a residual-on-data acquisition rule. I found no paper that sweeps mismatch strength for any of these acquisition rules.
- Williams et al. (2011), on design of field experiments under discrepancy, is still unread (not indexed; the user already notes this).

## Q2. Uncertainty that sees the missing term with few true points: which methods give coverage on the TRUE system from simulator data plus n true points, and has any been applied to physics surrogates?

### Takeaway
Synthetic-Powered Predictive Inference (SPI, Bashari, Lotan, Lee, Dobriban, Romano, arXiv:2505.13432) is the best fit. It treats abundant "synthetic" calibration data (here: nominal simulations, optionally plus sampled discrepancies) and m real points with a score transporter. It gives a worst-case coverage guardrail that holds whatever the synthetic distribution is, and near-nominal, tight sets when real and synthetic score distributions are close (the TV-distance term). Its slack term is a function of the real-vs-synthetic score mismatch, which the project's r dial controls directly. I found no application of SPI to physics surrogates. The other candidates are weaker for this use. TCC proves that label-free transfer must be near-vacuous in the worst case. Byun et al.'s CRC calibration relies on an assumed GP discrepancy family. Donati et al.'s directional conformal uses only real calibration data. PPI targets parameters (means, quantiles), not prediction sets, but could give a valid confidence interval on the true-system error or on r (useful for Q3).

### Cited Findings
- **SPI (arXiv:2505.13432, read).** Method: an empirical quantile-mapping "score transporter" T maps real scores onto the synthetic score scale using windows from order-statistic hypergeometric probabilities (level β), then calibrates on synthetic scores. Theorem 3.3: coverage lies in [1 − α − β − ε, 1 − α + β + ε + 1/(N+1)], where ε is the mean total-variation distance between the order-statistic distributions of real and synthetic scores. Theorem 3.5: worst-case bounds that depend only on (m, N, α, β) and hold for any synthetic distribution, "even when the synthetic data depend on the real calibration set". Corollary E.1: a separate score function s̃ may be used for the synthetic data. Experiments use as few as m = 15 real points (ImageNet with Stable Diffusion/FLUX images; MEPS tabular regression), β = 0.4, 100 trials. The paper notes that standard split conformal at m + 1 < 1/α must output trivial sets (for α = 0.05, m < 19). Code: github.com/Meshiba/spi. Stated limitations: marginal or label-conditional coverage only, not feature-conditional; i.i.d. real data assumed. — [arXiv:2505.13432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13432)
- **Doula, "Conformal Calibration Transfer" (TCC, arXiv:2609.10737, ICML 2026, read).** Setting: labelled calibration only in a source space, unlabelled source-target pairs, no target labels. Theorem 3.1: any procedure guaranteeing distribution-free target coverage from unlabelled target inputs alone must have worst-case expected set size ≥ (1 − α)|Y|, i.e. near-vacuous. TCC-KS builds a label-free mismatch certificate δ⁺ (one-sided KS gap on an uncertainty surrogate plus a DKW term) and tightens to α* = max(0, α − δ⁺). Coverage holds under an assumption that surrogate mismatch upper-bounds score mismatch (A2). Experiments: CIFAR-100-C, Tiny-ImageNet-C, SAR→RGB. Stated limitation: "shifts that do not register in such label-free signals are intrinsically difficult to detect without target labels". — [arXiv:2609.10737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10737)
- **Donati, Dabbene, Mammarella, "Directional Conformal UQ from Learned Model Discrepancy" (arXiv:2607.29344, eess.SY, read).** Nominal physics model kept online; a learned discrepancy δ̂ (kernel, NN or other) defines an asymmetric score S_dir = |d| / ((1 − ρ) + ρ·max(sign(d)·δ̃, 0)). Split-conformal validity holds whatever the accuracy of δ̂ ("influences only the shape of the band, never its validity"). An explicit improvement region |δ̃| ≤ τ is computable from calibration data. For RKHS discrepancy models, the power function makes ρ adaptive, reverting to the symmetric score where the discrepancy model has no support. Calibration uses real transitions only, with no simulator-data pooling. — [arXiv:2607.29344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29344)
- **Byun, Yoon, Chang, "Prediction Uncertainty Controlled Computer Model Calibration" (arXiv:2610.31163, read).** Amortised flow-matching posterior trained on simulator outputs plus GP-sampled discrepancies of varying structure; conformal risk control on a synthetic calibration set (n = 100) built by sampling fresh GP discrepancies. The guarantee rests on the working assumption that the real discrepancy lies in the GP family, which the authors say "cannot be verified from a single observation". Synthetic benchmark: bands about 20% of KOH-MCMC width at miscoverage 0.12 (target 0.10). Malawi measles SEIR: miscoverage 0.138 vs MCMC's 0.0345; miscovered weeks localise the structural inadequacy. They suggest a kernel two-sample test on residuals to flag exchangeability violations. — [arXiv:2610.31163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31163)
- **Angelopoulos et al., "Prediction-Powered Inference" (arXiv:2301.09633).** Appeared in search; PDF not opened this session. Its abstract describes valid inference when a small labelled dataset is supplemented with ML predictions. Variants found in search, **[abstract/title only]**: "Transfer Calibrated Prediction Powered Inference" (arXiv:2609.34156, Ohio State/Yale); "Multi-Source PPI" (arXiv:2606.21232); "A bootstrap approach to PPI" (arXiv:2606.28621, Stanford); "Learning a Size-Weight Frontier for Synthetic-Augmented Inference" (arXiv:2608.28576). — [arXiv:2301.09633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633)
- Other conformal-for-surrogates papers seen, **[abstract/title only]**: "How Synthetic Labels Improve Conformal Prediction: A Perspective on Conditional Coverage" (arXiv:2609.33482); "Local Conformal Predictions for Calibrated Surrogates" (arXiv:2607.01354, LHC amplitudes); "Multi-Granularity Conformal Prediction for Neural-Operator Automotive Aerodynamic Surrogates" (arXiv:2607.17297); "Threshold-Aware Conformal Routing" (arXiv:2610.02487, surrogate vs high-fidelity routing); "Multi-source conformal prediction: leveraging heterogeneity via localization" (arXiv:2609.14531). Judging by titles and snippets, these calibrate on data from the same simulator rather than on true-system data. — [arXiv:2609.33482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33482); [arXiv:2610.02487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02487)

### Inferences
- **Concrete consequence for the pre-registered RQ1 signal 5 (true-point conformal at n_cal ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64}).** At 90% coverage, split conformal needs ⌈(n+1)·0.9⌉ ≤ n, i.e. n ≥ 9. At n_cal = 8 the true-only interval is trivially infinite, and at n_cal = 16 it is set by the largest of the 16 scores. That is the regime where SPI's guarantee is most useful, and the claim can be checked against the order-statistic rule in the SPI paper.
- **Method idea: "discrepancy-augmented SPI".** Synthetic scores come from nominal simulations pushed through the corrected surrogate, plus discrepancies sampled from the discrepancy ensemble (an analogue of Byun et al.'s GP-augmentation, with the discrepancy learned from the n true points instead of assumed). SPI's Theorem 3.5 keeps the worst-case guardrail even though the synthetic set depends on the real points. Theorem 3.3 predicts efficiency degrading with the real-synthetic TV gap, which should grow with r for plain-nominal synthetic scores and stay flatter for discrepancy-augmented ones. That is a pre-registrable, falsifiable dose-response on the project's own dial.
- TCC's impossibility theorem justifies, citably, why true-system points are required at all (simulator-only calibration cannot be fixed with label-free signals). It strengthens the motivation for RQ1.
- Donati's directional score is a cheap optional add-on (one parameter ρ), but it competes for the same scarce true points as the discrepancy fit, so a sample split is needed. That cost is worst at n = 8.

### Gaps
- PPI (2301.09633) and its 2026 variants were not read in full. Whether a PPI or PPI++ CI on mean discrepancy norm stays valid with n = 8 and trajectory-valued outputs is unverified.
- I found no paper applying SPI or any synthetic-plus-real conformal method to a physics surrogate with a simulator-reality gap. The search was alphaXiv-only, so non-indexed venues (e.g. journals in computational mechanics) may have one.
- 2609.33482 (synthetic labels and conditional coverage) may give a conditional version that matters for "coverage where the missing term lives"; not read.

## Q3. Detection and diagnosis: can a handful of true points tell whether r is above the transition, and can "estimate r, then switch acquisition rule" be a practical adaptive method?

### Takeaway
Yes, and the setting makes it easier than generic sim-to-real detection. The nominal simulator can be run at every queried θ, so the discrepancy d(θ_i) = y_obs,i − f_nom(θ_i) is observed directly at each true point, up to known observation noise. The approximation error ‖f̂ − f_nom‖ can be computed at any θ for free. So r̂ only needs the n true points for its numerator, and a noise-debiased residual-energy test (LISDD-style sample-split F-test or a residual-energy-vs-noise-floor test) can be run after the first 8 points. Published analogues of a gate exist in other forms: TCC-KS's certificate δ⁺ adjusts conservatism; Parashar's β shrinks proxy weight under low correlation; Byun et al. use miscoverage clusters as a diagnostic. I found no paper that uses an estimated mismatch ratio to switch acquisition rules for a surrogate. The gate's value depends on H2 holding: if the arms do not diverge with r (as Koermer et al.'s GP result suggests may happen), the gate has nothing to gain.

### Cited Findings
- TCC-KS converts a measured, label-free mismatch statistic into an action (α* = max(0, α − δ⁺)), and the paper presents δ⁺ and ESS as "deployment signals" that "predict when correction is needed". — [arXiv:2609.10737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10737)
- Parashar et al.'s control-variate weight β = (k/(k+n))·Var(Y_s)⁻¹Cov(Y_r, Y_s) acts as a continuous data-driven trust weight on the simulator. With weak proxy-target correlation (nuPlan, ρ ≈ 0.15) it went to about 0, and the method reduced to target-only. — [arXiv:2608.13719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719)
- Byun et al. find that calibrated-interval failures localise structural inadequacy (asymmetric epidemic decline). They propose, but do not run, a kernel two-sample test on residual sequences to flag gross violations. — [arXiv:2610.31163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31163)
- LISDD (2606.23215, user's week-1 read) tests a candidate missing term with a sample-split F-test on nominal residuals of observed data. The user's notes flag its reported size 0.000 / power 1.000 as unusually clean. — [arXiv:2606.23215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23215)
- Tang et al.'s Fig. 10: the advantage of a misspecification-aware design over random shrinks as misspecification grows, with a fixed hyperparameter τ. The authors speculate τ should adapt to the degree of misspecification, which points toward a gate. — [arXiv:2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805)
- Two-sample-test and misspecification-diagnosis papers seen, **[abstract/title only]**: "PTED: A multi-dimensional two-sample test for scientific inference" (arXiv:2609.38388); "LOTTERY: two-sample testing under size asymmetry" (arXiv:2606.08460), relevant because n_true ≪ n_sim; "Unknown Unknowns: Model Misspecification in ML for Physics" (arXiv:2608.13633, Flatiron/SLAC); "When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification" (arXiv:2610.40770). — [arXiv:2606.08460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08460); [arXiv:2608.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13633)

### Inferences
- **Gate design (proposed arm 8, "r-gated acquisition").** After each round, compute r̂ = median_i(‖y_obs,i − f_nom(θ_i)‖ debiased by the known σ_obs) / median over the pool of ‖f̂(θ) − f_nom(θ)‖. Compute a bootstrap or PPI CI over the n true points. Use nominal σ (arm 3) while the CI's upper end is below r_switch; switch to arm 7 (or arm 5) once the lower end exceeds it. r_switch should not be tuned on main-grid results. The clean choice is r½ from H4 measured on the pilot seeds, or a fixed pre-registered value such as r = 1. This ties the method to the paper's measurement result: the measured transition becomes the decision rule.
- The gate statistic is nearly free. f_nom at queried θ is one 0.02 s solve, and ‖f̂ − f_nom‖ over the 4,096-point pool needs 4,096 nominal solves (about 90 s per round at the measured 0.0215 s per simulation; reusable across arms in a seed).
- The RLVR analogy holds structurally: like gating a training signal on a measured reliability statistic, the gate turns off a signal (simulator-only σ) once a measurement shows it no longer tracks the target. The analogy should stay out of the paper unless a reviewer audience in that area would care.
- What could make it fail: (i) H2 null, where arms 3 and 7 tie at every r and the gate cannot beat the best fixed arm; (ii) r̂ too noisy at n = 8 with 5% noise, so the gate flips late; (iii) arm 7 itself losing to Sobol (Romor et al.'s outlier effect), in which case the gate should switch to Sobol or arm 5 instead. A three-way gate (arm 3 → Sobol → arm 7) is safer, but each choice must be fixed in advance.

### Gaps
- The sampling distribution of r̂ at n = 8 under 1% and 5% noise is unknown; the pilot can measure it cheaply (no extra fits).
- I found no published adaptive-switching acquisition based on an estimated mismatch ratio. That supports novelty, but the search covered alphaXiv only, and the robotics sim-to-real literature (system identification for real-to-sim gap) was covered only through Parashar et al.

## Q4. Ranked shortlist of method contributions (closest prior work, delta, feasibility, pre-registration slot, risk)

### Takeaway
Ranked by (novelty gain × chance of a clean result) ÷ cost: (1) r-gated acquisition, (2) discrepancy-augmented SPI for true-system coverage with n ≤ 64 true points, (3) missing-term discovery on observed residuals feeding a repaired simulator, (4) an expected-total-error acquisition that combines nominal σ with the arm-7 discrepancy estimate, (5) directional conformal from the learned discrepancy (optional). Ideas 1-2 are cheap enough for the late-January workshop cut. Idea 3 is the strongest method but changes the paper's framing, so it belongs in the full paper.

### Cited Findings
**1. r-gated acquisition (gate on estimated mismatch ratio), proposed arm 8.**
- Closest: TCC-KS mismatch certificate → action [arXiv:2609.10737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10737); control-variate trust weight β [arXiv:2608.13719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719); LISDD residual F-test [arXiv:2606.23215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23215); R-IDeA's misspecification-dependent hyperparameter [arXiv:2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805).
- Delta: none of these switches the acquisition rule for a surrogate based on a measured ratio of structural to approximation error, and none sets the switch point from a measured transition (H4).
- Feasibility: very high, since it reuses arms 3/5/7 code. Extra RQ2 fits: 2 systems × 5 ε × 10 seeds × 6 rounds × 5 members = 3,000 (about +12% on the 25,500 planned; derived from the user's own compute arithmetic).
- Experiment that proves it: per seed, budget-AUC regret of arm 8 relative to the per-r best fixed arm, compared with each fixed arm's regret, across all ε. Success means arm 8 has lower mean regret than every fixed arm (it never loses badly anywhere).
- Risk: if H2 is null, regret is about 0 for everyone. Report it as "no harm, no gain".

**2. Discrepancy-augmented SPI (true-system coverage from simulations plus n true points).**
- Closest: SPI [arXiv:2505.13432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13432); GP-discrepancy-augmented CRC [arXiv:2610.31163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31163); directional conformal [arXiv:2607.29344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29344); TCC impossibility [arXiv:2609.10737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10737).
- Delta: first use of synthetic-powered conformal with a physics simulator as the synthetic source. The synthetic set is augmented with discrepancies learned from the same n true points; SPI's Theorem 3.5 allows this dependence. The r dial gives a controlled test of the TV-gap term in Theorem 3.3.
- Feasibility: very high, post-hoc on RQ1 models with near-zero fits; public code.
- Experiment: RQ1 cells × n_cal ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64}. Compare true-only split conformal, sim-only conformal (signal 4), plain SPI and augmented SPI on true-test coverage, width, and the slope of width against log r.
- Risk: trajectory-norm scores may make the transporter windows wide at m = 8. If the r-growth in TV gap is slight, the plain-vs-augmented difference may be small.

**3. Missing-term discovery on observed residuals → repaired simulator ("physics repair" arm).**
- Closest: UDE+symbolic-regression T-optimal design [arXiv:2604.01231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01231); E-SINDy active learning [arXiv:2606.12182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12182); LISDD [arXiv:2606.23215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23215).
- Delta: sparse regression of the arm-7 residual (spline derivatives) on a term library. The identified term is added to the nominal simulator, which is then re-simulated at unlimited θ to retrain the surrogate, so the correction comes from cheap simulations rather than from 56 points. Acquisition uses E-SINDy coefficient disagreement. Compared across r against the data-driven discrepancy arms.
- Feasibility: moderate. Library fits are cheap, but each surrogate refit is an ensemble at n_nom = 512; refitting only at rounds 1, 3 and 6 cuts this to about 1,500 fits.
- Experiment: must include a misspecified-library condition (e.g. S2 with δ unknown, or a library lacking the exact form), otherwise a reviewer will call it rigged.
- Risk: in a pendulum toy with the true term in the library, this arm may dominate everything. That is useful, but it turns the paper toward "repair the simulator" and away from the measurement study.

**4. Expected-total-error acquisition (nominal σ² ⊕ predicted discrepancy from arm 7 / arm 5).**
- Closest: E-SINDy "model error × disagreement" score [arXiv:2606.12182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12182); control-variate proxy-corrected acquisition [arXiv:2608.13719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13719); R-IDeA de-amplification [arXiv:2506.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07805).
- Delta: an additive score matched to the project's e = a + d decomposition, for neural surrogates.
- Feasibility: high, with +3,000 fits as for idea 1.
- Risk: error-seeking scores oversample outliers at small budgets (Romor et al. 2610.27130, in the user's notes). Incremental unless it clearly beats arms 5/7.

**5. Directional conformal from the learned discrepancy (optional RQ1 signal).**
- Closest: [arXiv:2607.29344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29344).
- Delta: a nominal neural surrogate in place of a nominal control model; mismatch sweep.
- Feasibility: high, post-hoc.
- Risk: needs a split of the scarce true points between discrepancy fit and calibration, so at n = 8 it is likely no better than symmetric.

### Inferences
- **Pre-registration slot.** The pilot has not run, so an amendment is still clean. Write ideas 1 and 2 (and 3 if kept) as a new section, "Extensions (pre-registered 2026-11-xx, before pilot)", committed as tag `prereg-v1.1` and logged in section 11 with the reason. Put them in a new Family C with its own Holm correction, leaving Families A and B unchanged so the original confirmatory claims keep their power. Suggested Family C:
  - C1: arm 8 regret < min fixed-arm regret, per system, paired t on per-seed values.
  - C2: augmented-SPI coverage ≥ 0.9 − tolerance and width < true-only conformal at n_cal ∈ {8, 16}.
  - C3: slope of SPI width against log r is smaller for augmented than for plain SPI.
  - The r_switch rule for arm 8 is frozen in the amendment (e.g. r½ from the pilot seeds, or r = 1).
- **Deadline trade-off (per the user's standing instruction to flag scope/budget decisions).**
  - *What matters:* the workshop paper needs one clean result. A method arm only helps if the confirmatory results (H2/H4) show arms diverging with r; Koermer et al.'s GP result is prior evidence that they may not. In that case the gate's most likely outcome is "ties the best arm", a modest, honest result.
